  • INTL400: The Eagle and the Condor: U.S. Intervention and Hemispheric Conformity in Cold War Latin America

    This case exists within the wider context of the United States’ implementation of interventionist policies around the world during the Cold War era, and it reflects the U.S. government’s hemispheric strategy to enforce ideological conformity across Latin America. Part of the extension of this strategy was Operation Condor, an interstate intelligence system comprising various Southern Cone countries and supported indirectly by the U.S. government and the Central Intelligence Agency. Existing literature examines U.S. interventions in countries such as Guatemala and Chile, often framed through the National Security Doctrine, which guided Washington’s Cold War objectives in the region. Using qualitative evidence from declassified government documents and quantitative data from the School of the Americas Watch dataset, this study identifies three patterns of American interventionist elements present in Condor: ideological enforcement, military indoctrination, and power leveraging. This case analyzes declassified CIA documents released in 2019, revealing Washington’s extensive knowledge and participation in the transnational campaign of terror waged by Operation Condor states. These documents demonstrate how the United States enforced anti-communist ideology in Latin America and Argentina through the identified patterns. By focusing on an under-studied country, this case addresses existing gaps on Operation Condor and U.S. intervention, illustrating the thematic mechanisms through which the policies were executed.
